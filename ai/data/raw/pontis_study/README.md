# Data from: A large cohort study of postnatal events over 18 months in a not-for-profit referral centre in Vellore, South India

Dataset DOI: [10.5061/dryad.wh70rxwz3](10.5061/dryad.wh70rxwz3)

## Description of the data and file structure

The assessment at these contact points was done by health care providers (HCP) /research staff with a nursing or allied health background, with a minimum of 5-10 years of experience in collecting data for research projects. Trained field workers were involved in contacting women and children, checking weight, blood pressure^,^ and hemoglobin using a point-of-care test.   Good clinical practice certification was ensured for all research staff involved with the study. Interviews at each contact were conducted by a different HCP as a strategy to prevent underreporting of events. Privacy was ensured at all times during the interview. The interviews facilitated the completion of the postnatal health assessment questionnaire (PNHAQ) using the RedCap software system.  In one of these visits at the face-to-face assessment, the mother’s weight, blood pressure, haemoglobin, and general well-being were checked. Blood pressure was checked using an automated instrument (Omron model HEM 8712-Omron Health Care-India Private Limited-Vietnam). Weight was checked with a Nova weighing machine (Model PS 126-Health Sense Bangalore, Karnataka). These machines were regularly checked for accuracy. Women who attended the parent hospita, had hemoglobin checked with an automated spectrometric analyser, and those who had an in-person visit at home had spot hemoglobin estimation using a haemoglobinometer (HaemCue HB 301 Analyser, which was factory calibrated against an international reference). Anaemia was diagnosed when the hemoglobin was less than 10gm/dl. Postnatal hypertension was diagnosed when the BP exceeded 140/90 mmHg. Maternal mental health assessment was done using the Edinburgh postnatal depression scale (EPDS) and Perceived Stress Scale (PSS) as mentioned in the protocol. Similarly, only at one of the contacts, the baby’s weight and development delay were measured using the Trivandrum Developmental Screening Chart. The details of the baby were also filled in PNHAQ. The women who were lost to follow-up were traced with a minimum of 10 phone calls in order to ensure optimal tracking.

### Files and variables

PoNTiS_Study(1).csv: Contains assessment response for the below mentioned file.

PoNTiS_Study(2).csv: This list contains the standard components of a postnatal assessment form. It gathers information on the mother’s medical, obstetric, surgical, psychological, and socioeconomic background, as well as details about delivery, complications, and recovery. It also includes assessments of the newborn’s health, breastfeeding, contraception plans, mental health screening, and follow-up visits. Overall, it helps evaluate the wellbeing of both mother and baby during the postnatal period.

Code/software: RedCap Software


## Human subjects data

Human subject data have been de-identified in accordance with applicable regulations. All identifiable information has been removed or anonymised to protect participant privacy.
